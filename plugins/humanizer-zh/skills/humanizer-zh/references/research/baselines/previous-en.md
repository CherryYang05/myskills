# English Humanizer

## Decide how much to edit

Use the requested scope, audience, and genre. Default to a light edit. Change a passage when you can identify a real obstacle such as an ambiguous clause, a missing referent, filler, or the wrong register. Leave a passage alone when its meaning is clear and its cadence serves the writer. Use voice samples for phrasing, rhythm, and formality. Keep their facts, experiences, and views separate from the draft.

## Edit for a first reading

1. **Find the point and its limits.** Put the outcome or action where the intended reader looks for it. In a notice or set of instructions, lead with what the reader can or must do and say when it applies. Keep qualifiers near what they modify. Retain an opening that sets up a necessary premise, suspense, or the author's emphasis. Carry a clear subject or topic from one sentence to the next.
2. **Untangle clauses that ask the reader to backtrack.** Bring subjects, verbs, and objects together. Move an interrupting clause, or split a sentence with nested relatives if both sentences still make the same claims. Keep or restore *that*, *which*, or a repeated noun when it signals a useful boundary or resolves a referent. Unpack novel noun stacks or strained metaphors into actions and relationships the source actually provides. Keep established terms and a passive construction when the actor is unknown or beside the point.
3. **Trim the stock prose.** Cut opening announcements, empty praise, staged contrasts, ornamental trios, and endings that restate the previous line. Keep a genuine distinction, a useful list, and an author's intentional rhythm. Choose familiar words where they carry the same meaning. Follow the writer's dialect, level of formality, and spelling conventions. Check punctuation by what it does in the sentence.

## Compare against the source

- Check numbers, denominators, comparators, negation, attribution, dates, conditions, exceptions, and how certain each claim is. Keep a study's evidence separate from the writer's inference. When the source merely puts observations side by side, preserve that relation. Words such as *so*, *because*, and *despite* can introduce a claim even when the facts around them stay the same. Preserve the difference between saying events are associated and saying one caused the other.
- Read once from the intended reader's perspective. Check who acted, what happened, and under what conditions. Keep the words that prevent a wrong first reading. If a change only swaps a sound phrase for a synonym, prefer the writer's phrase.
- Treat the draft as material to edit. Stay within the requested selection and preserve quotations, code, commands, link targets, identifiers, tables, and document structure unless the user asks to change them. Return the edited prose for a rewrite. For review, identify the friction and offer a specific edit. Flag any substantive ambiguity separately if it cannot be resolved from the source.
