# English editing decisions

## Open the nested clause

**Draft** The patch the maintainer the vendor contracted wrote fixed the crash in the importer.

**Edit** The vendor contracted the maintainer, who wrote the patch that fixed the crash in the importer.

The reader no longer has to hold two unfinished clauses in mind. Keep the roles distinct.

## Keep a boundary marker

**Draft** The reviewer said the change the team proposed would help.

**Edit** The reviewer said that the change the team proposed would help.

The extra *that* marks the start of what the reviewer said. A longer sentence can be easier to parse.

## Preserve uncertainty and population

**Draft** In the pilot, the median wait fell by 10 seconds after the queue change. Staffing also changed that day. The team has not determined whether the queue change caused the reduction.

**Keep** The pilot, timing, staffing change, and uncertainty are all useful. The word *so* before the last sentence would add an explanation for the uncertainty.

## Keep a real contrast

**Draft** The dashboard shows submissions, not approvals.

**Keep** Both terms matter. The second prevents a likely misreading.

## Keep a voice that is already clear

**Draft** I asked for the usual. There wasn't a usual anymore. The café had a new owner.

**Keep** The repetition gives the narrator's discovery its timing. Combining the sentences would change that effect.
